## Shorthand used here

| word | what it means |
|---|---|
| **foul** | what a player really did: a hold, a jump across the line, a late hit. It is a game fact whether or not anyone saw it |
| **flag** | what the officials called. A flag can be on a real foul, or on nothing (a **wrong flag**) |
| **seen chance** | how likely the officials are to flag a real foul of this kind |
| **visibility** | how plain a foul is to see: a false start is plain, a hold in the pile is not |
| **crew** | the seven officials who work a game together, led by the referee. The NFL has 17 |
| **crew lean** | how much more or less a crew calls one family of fouls than the league |
| **enforce** | mark off the penalty by the rulebook: the yards, the spot, the down |
| **free play** | the offense keeps the play's result or takes the penalty, whichever is better (ADR-091) |
| **called rate** | flags per 1,000 snaps; this is all the real data shows |
| **true rate** | fouls per 1,000 snaps, flagged or not; the data cannot show it |

## 1. The need

Nothing produces a penalty today. `PENALTY` is in the contracts (`contracts/src/events.ts:531`, CHECKED) with a four-field payload, and `penalty_check` is a `CheckKind` (`events.ts:147`, CHECKED) with no producer (`engine/src/game/events.ts:60`, CHECKED). The engine's own comments name penalties as missing (`engine/src/sim/passPlay.ts:9`, `runPlay.ts:22`, `specialTeams.ts:19`, `game/types.ts:311`, CHECKED).

A real NFL game has **12.8 flags on scrimmage snaps** and 1.2 on kicks; each team has **5.3 enforced a game for about 43 yards**, plus 3.6 on kicks (COMPUTED, real-numbers page). That is more yards than most teams' longest drive, and it moves first downs, field goals and touchdowns. The game plays without any of it.

The owner has asked for two things, and they are two layers:

1. **The rules are the NFL's.** Every foul carries the penalty the NFL enforces, at the NFL's spot, with the NFL's down and first-down rules (§4).
2. **The officials are human.** They miss fouls, they sometimes flag what was not there, and crews differ (§5, §6). So the game must hold **what happened** and **what was called** apart, both on the event stream (Iron Rule 3).

## 2. The design in brief

1. **Two layers.** The players commit fouls (a `FOUL` fact, rolled from the moment that causes it, through the player's **Discipline**). The officials decide whether to flag it (a `PENALTY`, rolled from the foul's visibility and the crew). A flag on nothing comes from a play that **nearly** was a foul (§5.3).
2. **The NFL's rulebook enforces every flag** (§4): the yards, the spot (previous spot, spot of the foul, end of the run, after the play), half the distance to the goal line, automatic first downs and loss of down only where the NFL gives them, a safety for an offensive foul in its own end zone, offsetting fouls, the offended team's option, the 10-second runoff, the untimed down.
3. **Three kinds of visibility** (§5.2): **plain** (before the snap and the obvious ones), **in the open** (pass interference, hits on the quarterback), **in the crowd** (holding, hands to the face, contact downfield). How much of the crowd the officials see is the owner's question **Q1**; how often they flag nothing is **Q2**.
4. **17 crews** with their own leans by family of foul, sized to the real spread between the 17 NFL referees (§6.1): crews really do differ on holding (the strictest calls 3.4 offensive holds a game, the loosest 2.1) and hardly at all on pass interference. Whether they are named and known in game week is **Q3**.
5. **The situation** (§6.4): real flags fall about 16% late in close games and 17% in the playoffs. Whether officials "let them play" is **Q4**. The home team's edge is **Q5**.
6. **Fouls nobody flagged are on film** (§7): whether the coach sees them is **Q6**.
7. **The build** (§11): the rulebook and the officials first, inert (nothing calls them), which may be built during the pocket fix's measuring if the owner says so (**Q7**); then each family of fouls with the design that produces it, every step measured against the real called rates.

## 3. Who does what

| who | what | ratings read (through the registry) |
|---|---|---|
| **the player** | commits the foul, or does not, at the moment that tempts him (a lost block, a beaten route, a late hit) | **Discipline** (every foul); **Volatility** (personal fouls); the moment's own ratings decide whether the moment happens at all |
| **the officials** | see the foul or not; flag a near-foul or not | the foul's **visibility** (§5.2); the **crew's lean** for that family (§6.1); the situation (§6.4) |
| **the head coach** (COACH authority, Iron Rule 6) | accepts or declines each flag against the other team; picks between options (a free play, a 10-second runoff) | none: it is a decision. For a team the computer runs, the rule in §4.6 |
| **the referee** | announces; enforces by the rulebook | none: enforcement is the rulebook, not a roll |

**Why Discipline.** It is the game's rating for staying inside the coach's instructions when his own read says otherwise (`registry.ts:130`, every position, CHECKED), and `attributes.md` §3's L3 menu already names "pre-snap penalty rates → awareness/discipline" as where it will be derived from (CHECKED). A player who holds a lot has low Discipline; that is the rating's meaning, not a new one. **Personal fouls also read personality**: `player-personality.md` names unsportsmanlike and taunting rates as a sign of volatility, so personal fouls also read the player's **Volatility** (`registry.ts:127`, every position, CHECKED): a volatile player's late hits and taunts rise (§8, step 6).

### 4.2 The spots

- **The spots of enforcement** (3-13-3, 14-3-4). **Previous spot:** where the ball was last put in play; on a scrimmage down, where it was snapped. **Spot of the foul:** where the foul was committed; for intentional grounding, where the passer threw (8-2-1). **Spot of a fumble or backward pass:** where it happened (two end-zone exceptions, 14-4-6 Notes). **Dead-ball spot:** where the ball became dead: a runner's forward progress, however far the runner is then pushed or carried back (3-12-1); the forward point of the ball where it went out of bounds (3-20-4); or in an end zone. **Succeeding spot:** where the ball will next be put in play: where the penalty leaves it, or, with no foul, the dead-ball spot; after an incomplete pass, the previous spot (8-1-4). **Spot of a change of possession:** where the other team gained the ball or was awarded it. (The other Try spot comes with the try.) A foul **before the snap** is marked from the succeeding spot, where the ball was to be snapped, and the down stays the same; a foul **at the snap** from the previous spot, and the down is repeated; each unless the penalty gives a first down (14-4-1). A foul **after the play** is marked from the succeeding spot, and the down counts (14-4-9). The **basic spot** of a run is the dead-ball spot; when the run is followed by a change of possession, the spot where possession was lost; for a foul during a fumble or backward pass, the spot of the fumble or pass (14-3-5). "End of the run" is not one of the rulebook's spots; 8-5's penalty uses the phrase once (§4.3 note 7). The one spot of a foul the rulebook sets by rule is the 20, for the receiving team's post-possession foul in its own end zone on a scrimmage kick (14-4-8); it comes with kicks. **CORRECTED:** the 2026-10-31 row named four spots; the rulebook lists seven, a foul before the snap is marked from the succeeding spot (where the ball was to be snapped, the yard line the old rows called the previous spot), and "the end of the run" is the basic spot or the dead-ball spot.
- **The basic spot and the three-and-one method** (14-3-5, 14-3-6, 14-4-2 to 14-4-6). **CORRECTED:** the 2026-10-31 row marked three fouls from spots that are the previous spot (14-3-6): the defense's foul on a run that ended behind the line ("from the line"), the offense's foul behind the line (the spot of the foul), and the offense's foul beyond the line on a run that ended behind it (the basic spot). On a pass play it gave the previous spot to the offense's fouls only before the pass, where 14-4-5 gives it to every foul by either team until the pass ends. It left out turnovers and the pass play's exceptions, and gave roughing the passer a spot of its own.
  - **A run** (3-24-3; a catch starts one, 14-4-5). The defense's foul: from the basic spot wherever it happened, but from the previous spot when the basic spot is behind the line. The offense's foul: from the previous spot when the foul is behind the line or the basic spot is behind the line; otherwise from the spot of the foul when the foul is behind the basic spot, and from the basic spot when it is beyond it. The offense's foul in the defense's end zone before it scores: from the goal line (14-3-6). Behind its own goal line: the last bullet below.
  - **A run followed by a change of possession** (14-4-3; a failed fourth down is one, 3-35-3, §17): accepting the defense's foul gives the ball back to the offense first, and of several such fouls the one best for the offense is taken. The defense keeps the ball only by declining the offense's foul, except the offense's personal or unsportsmanlike conduct foul: then the defense keeps the ball, and the yards come off from the dead-ball spot (from the spot where possession changed, if it loses the ball again, and it still keeps it).
  - **A fumble or backward pass** (14-4-6): its spot is the basic spot. Behind the line, every foul by either team is marked from the previous spot (the offense's in its own end zone is a safety if the defense elects). The offense's personal or unsportsmanlike conduct foul during one the defense recovers: from the dead-ball spot, and the defense keeps the ball (from the spot of its recovery if it loses the ball again, and it still keeps it). The text applies the same to one the offense recovers short of the line to gain; on downs one to three that is OPEN in the rulebook (step 0 has no fumbles or backward passes, §17).
  - These line-of-scrimmage rules apply from scrimmage; a return after a change of possession has no line of scrimmage (3-18-1, 3-29).
  - **A pass play** (14-4-5): from the snap until a forward pass thrown from behind the line is caught or falls incomplete, every foul by either team is marked from the previous spot; a run before the pass is part of the pass play (14-4-2 Note), and once the pass is caught a run begins. Exceptions: (1) intentional grounding (8-2-1; §4.3); (2) the defense's pass interference, at the spot of the foul (8-5 Penalty; §4.3); (3) the offense's foul behind its own goal line, a safety if the defense elects (the last bullet below); (4) the defense's personal or unsportsmanlike conduct foul before a completion: from the previous spot or the dead-ball spot, whichever is better for the offense; if the offense completes the pass and then loses the ball, it keeps the ball, and the yards come from the previous spot; if it scores, the yards go to the try (refused at step 0; step 6 does it (§17)); (5) the offense's personal or unsportsmanlike conduct foul before it loses the ball: from the dead-ball spot, and the defense keeps the ball (from the spot of the defense's catch or recovery if it loses the ball again, and it still keeps it); the text applies this also to such a foul before a pass thrown from behind the line "when the offense fails to make the line to gain", which on downs one to three is OPEN in the rulebook; after an incomplete pass the ball returns to the previous spot (8-1-4), so step 0 marks the foul from there either way, the down repeated (14-1-2 Item 1), and a catch downed short of the line to gain is refused at step 0; step 6 does it (§17; §4.3 note 11); on a failed fourth down with the pass in the air, whether the offense is "losing possession during a pass play" is OPEN in the rulebook; step 0 reads it as not, and offers the previous spot with the offense's ball or 14-2-4's dead-ball spot with the defense's (§17; §4.3 note 11); (6) a hit on a defenseless intended receiver during or just after an interception: enforced after the interception, and the intercepting team keeps the ball (12-2-9); the caller flags it as after the catch, so it is refused at step 0; the step that builds returns does it (§17). A pass thrown from beyond the line, or with no line of scrimmage, is a foul during a run (14-4-5 Notes); on such a play, another flag during the play is refused at step 0; step 7 does it (§17; §4.3 note 8).
  - **Any personal or unsportsmanlike conduct foul** not part of a double foul may also be marked from the dead-ball spot when the offended team has the ball at the end of the down (14-2-4). Step 0 offers this spot for the offense's fouls: before an interception, on a failed fourth-down run and before the throw on one it is the rule's own spot (14-4-3, 14-4-5), so it is a choice of its own only while the pass is in the air on a failed fourth down; for the defense's it is never better for the offense than the spots above in step 0's plays (CHOSEN, §17).
  - **An unsportsmanlike conduct foul other than taunting, at the snap or during the play:** 12-3-1's two penalty paragraphs mark its acts from different spots, and a flag does not say which act it was: refused at step 0; step 6 does it (§17; §4.3 note 12).
- **Half the distance** (14-2-1): when a distance penalty would move the ball more than half the distance from its spot of enforcement to the offending team's goal line, the penalty is half that distance; exactly half is not more than half. It overrides every other enforcement of a distance penalty: dead-ball fouls, personal fouls marked from the dead-ball spot and the 15 yards added to pass interference included. It is measured from the rulebook's own spot of enforcement. Two exceptions (14-2-1). Intentional grounding goes by its own penalty (8-2-1): a loss of down at the spot of the pass, never cut, when that spot is more than 10 yards behind the previous spot or more than half the distance to the goal line; a safety when the passer is in their own end zone as they throw; otherwise 10 yards from the previous spot, and whether those 10 are cut when they would be more than half the distance is OPEN in the rulebook; step 0 reads it as cut to half the distance (§17; §4.3 note 9). A palpably unfair act: the Referee sets the distance (12-3-4). The defense's pass interference is a spot foul: in the field of play the ball goes to the spot of the foul, however far that is; in the end zone it is first down at the 1, or halfway to the goal line when the ball was snapped inside the 2 (8-5 Penalty, 14-4-5). **CORRECTED:** added the exceptions, the spot it is measured from, and what it binds; ADR-091 §8 already had the rule.
- **A foul by the offense behind its own goal line during the down** is a safety if the defense elects to enforce it at that spot (3-28, 11-5-1, 14-2-2, 14-3-6, 14-4-5, 14-4-6): holding, illegal use of hands, a block in the back, a personal foul; an illegal forward pass thrown from its end zone (a second pass, or one thrown after the ball had been beyond the line, 14-4-5 Notes); and intentional grounding when the passer is in the end zone as they throw, whose penalty is the safety itself (8-2-1). On a run whose dead-ball spot is in its own end zone, any foul by the offense is a safety, wherever it happened (14-3-6). Whether the defense, instead of the safety, may take the general rule's spot (the previous spot, 14-3-6): OPEN in the rulebook; step 0 reads it as three choices, the safety, the general rule's spot or decline (§17). When the defense ends the down with the ball, the offense's personal foul goes by §4.3 note 11 instead: on a failed fourth-down run, and while the pass is in the air on one, the safety stands beside note 11's accepts; before an interception, and before the throw on a failed fourth down, step 0 offers no safety, only note 11's one accept, the defense's ball, or decline (§17). Not a safety: the offense's foul after the play, marked from the succeeding spot (14-4-9). After a change of possession, the team that gained the ball is the offense (3-35-1). When the ball is dead in its own end zone after the other team's impetus, its foul after the change goes by 14-4-4: in the end zone, a safety if the player tried to advance, else from the 20; in the field of play, from the spot of the foul or the 20, whichever is worse for it. With its own impetus, any foul of its is a safety. When a player's momentum carried them into their own end zone, a live-ball foul by their team there is a safety, and one in the field of play goes by the three-and-one from the spot of the change of possession (11-5-1, 14-4-4 Note). Live-ball fouls after a change of possession: refused at step 0; the step that builds returns does them (§17). A dead-ball foul after a touchback: OPEN in the rulebook (14-4-4 speaks of "a foul by either team after a change of possession"); step 0 reads 14-4-4 as being about live-ball fouls, and marks a dead-ball foul from the succeeding spot, the 20 (14-4-9) (§17). The defense's foul whose spot of enforcement is behind the offense's goal line is marked from the goal line, or from the 20 when the play ends in a touchback; the offense's foul whose spot is behind the defense's goal line, from the goal line (14-2-2). **CORRECTED:** grounding is a safety only when thrown from the end zone; the row adds the defense's election, the run that ends in the end zone, the foul after the play, and fouls behind the other goal line.

1. **Defensive offside.** In the last two minutes of a half, with the offense legally set, a defender not on their side of the line when the ball is snapped commits a neutral zone infraction: the whistle blows at once, there is no play and no free play, and it is enforced as that row (7-4-4). Step 0 takes only the neutral zone infraction there (§17). In overtime too: OPEN in the rulebook; step 0 reads it as applying, since overtime has the fourth period's timing (16-1-3) (§17).
2. **Delay of game (offense).** Before the snap, when the play clock runs out (4-6-1, 4-6-4), the ball stays dead, and the down and the line to gain stay the same: after a first-down play, first-and-15 in the open field, and goal to go stays goal to go (14-4-1 Item 1, 14-1-2 Item 7, 14-4-9 Item 1). After a down (the ball spiked or thrown in the field of play, a player staying on the ball or on a downed runner, 4-6-5): from where the next play starts, after the play's result, and the down counts; first-and-10 when that down made a first down (14-4-9 Item 1). From the previous spot only when the ball was in play (4-6 Penalty). A second delay in the same down with time in is unsportsmanlike conduct, 15 yards (12-3-1 (n)); step 0 takes each delay as a first, and step 3 does the second (§17).
3. **Illegal formation, illegal shift, illegal motion.** After the two-minute warning with the game clock running, offensive players not all set together for one full second before the snap commit a false start (a dead ball, as that row, with §4.5's runoff), not an illegal shift (7-4-2 Item 6, 7-4-6). Step 0 takes only the false start there (§17). In overtime too: OPEN in the rulebook; step 0 reads it as applying (16-1-3) (§17).
4. **Too many men on the field.** At the snap: more than 11 on the field when the ball is snapped (5-1-1). Before the snap: more than 11 in the formation, the offense's for more than three seconds or the defense's with the snap imminent, or 12 or more in the offense's huddle while the play clock runs (5-1-1, 5-2-1). When the offense snaps before the defense could match its substitutions (after an offensive substitution, a timeout or a change of possession) and the defense has too many men, there is no penalty except for personal fouls and unsportsmanlike conduct, and the down is replayed; after an offensive substitution, the game clock also goes back to the time of the snap and starts on the snap (5-2-9, 5-2-10). Step 3 builds the quick snap (§17).
5. **Offensive holding, illegal use of hands (offense), illegal block in the back.** The basic spot is the dead-ball spot; where the runner lost the ball when the other team gains it; the spot of a fumble or backward pass for a foul during one (14-3-5). From the goal line when the foul is in the defense's end zone before a touchdown (14-3-6). When the other team gains the ball, it keeps it only by declining (14-4-3, 14-4-5). On a return, with no line of scrimmage, the plain three-and-one applies (14-3-6, 3-18-1, 3-29). Live-ball fouls during a return: refused at step 0; the step that builds returns does them (§17), and a block in the back by the team that gained the ball comes with it. After the yards, the down is replayed when the ball is behind the line to gain and it is first-and-10 when the ball is in advance of it (14-1-2 Items 1 and 4). Exactly on the line to gain: OPEN in the rulebook; step 0 reads it as first-and-10 (§17).
6. **Defensive holding, illegal use of hands (defense), illegal contact.** A pass play runs from the snap until a pass thrown from behind the line is caught or falls incomplete, a scramble before the pass included (14-4-5, 14-4-2 Note). A play with no forward pass from behind the line, such as a sack or a scramble with no throw, is a run (3-24-3). On a run, the basic spot is the dead-ball spot; when the run ends with the other team gaining the ball, the spot where possession was lost, and the ball goes back to the offense first (14-4-3); for a foul while a fumble or backward pass is loose, its spot (14-3-5). Illegal contact is a foul only while the passer is in the pocket with the ball: the restriction ends when the passer "throws a forward or backward pass" or leaves the pocket with the ball (8-4-7), so on a pass play step 0 takes it only before the throw, and on a run only on a sack or a scramble (§17).
7. **Defensive pass interference.** In the end zone: first down at the defense's 1 when the ball was snapped on or outside the defense's 2; when it was snapped inside the 2, halfway from the previous spot to the goal line, first down (8-5 Penalty, 14-4-5; TO CHECK (1), below). When the interference is also a personal foul, both may be enforced: the personal foul's 15 yards from the spot of the interference, or "from the end of the run" if the interference is declined (8-5 Penalty, 14-1-3). "The end of the run" is not one of the rulebook's spots (§4.2): OPEN in the rulebook; step 0 reads it as 14-4-5's spot for the defense's personal foul before a completion, the previous spot or the dead-ball spot, whichever is better for the offense (14-4-5) (§17). Half the distance binds those 15 yards (14-2-1). The same act happens during the play with the ball in the air, the time pass interference rules apply (8-5-1: "from the time the ball is thrown until the ball is touched"); a personal foul at another moment is a foul of its own, and a taunt, a dead-ball foul whenever it happens (14-4-9), is never that act (§17).
8. **Illegal forward pass.** Thrown from beyond the line: the passer's whole body and the ball are past the line as they let go, and the play is a run (8-1-2, 14-4-5 Notes). Loss of down (14-1-2 Item 2): on fourth down the yards are marked off and the ball goes to the defense; when the yards leave the ball beyond the line to gain, first-and-10 on downs one to three (14-1-2 Item 4). On fourth down with the ball left beyond the line to gain: OPEN in the rulebook (Item 2 gives the ball to the defense, Item 4 a first down); refused at step 0; step 7 does it (§17). Another flag during the play, on a play with a pass thrown from beyond the line: OPEN in the rulebook (where a run that ends in a pass has its basic spot, 14-3-5 against 3-24-3); refused at step 0; step 7 does it (§17). A flag at the snap is still marked from the previous spot (14-4-1 Item 2), and a dead-ball flag from the succeeding spot (14-4-9). A forward pass thrown after a change of possession: 5 yards from the spot of the pass, no loss of down, first-and-10 for the team with the ball (8-1-2, 14-1-2 Item 6). A pass released on or beyond the defense's goal line names no play: the passer has already scored (11-2), so a caller must never pass it either (§17).
9. **Intentional grounding.** At the spot of the pass the yards are never cut (14-2-1, 8-2-1). When neither spot-of-the-pass condition holds and 10 yards would be more than half the distance to the goal line, the rulebook does not say whether the 10 are cut (14-2-1 points to 8-2-1, which is silent): OPEN in the rulebook; step 0 reads it as cut to half the distance (§17). When 10 yards would be more than half the distance, a pass thrown exactly half the distance back meets neither spot-of-the-pass condition ("more than half the distance", 8-2-1), so it is the 10 cut to half the distance, which reaches the same yard line (§17). After the two-minute warning with time in, grounding also brings the runoff (4-7-1, §4.5). "The pass does not have to be incomplete" (8-2-1); step 0 takes grounding only on an incomplete pass (§17).
10. **Roughing the passer.** The dead-ball spot is the better one on a completed pass downed beyond the line, measured where the ball finally became dead (14-4-5). If the offense completes the pass and then loses the ball, it keeps the ball, and the yards come from the previous spot; after an incomplete pass (the ball goes back to the previous spot, 8-1-4) or an interception, the yards come from the previous spot, and the offense keeps the ball (14-4-5; for an interception, as 14-4-3 and 12-2-9 imply). If the offense scores, the yards go to the try (14-4-5, 14-2-3): refused at step 0; step 6 does it (§17). A hit on the passer after an interception is a foul by the team then holding the ball: it keeps the ball, the 15 yards are marked against it, and it gets first-and-10 (12-2-11, 14-1-2 Item 6): refused at step 0; the step that builds returns does it (§17). A hit on a passer who threw from beyond the line is not roughing the passer, though unnecessary roughness may apply (8-1-2 Notes), and it is a foul during a run (14-4-5 Notes); on a sack, or a scramble with no throw, the play is a run (3-24-3) and the three-and-one applies (14-3-6). A hit after the ball is dead is a dead-ball foul (note 11).
11. **Unnecessary roughness, face mask, horse-collar tackle, clipping, chop block; tripping.** During a run (a catch starts one), or while a fumble or backward pass is loose, the three-and-one (§4.2). The defense's foul: from the basic spot, or from the previous spot when the basic spot is behind the line; after a turnover, accepting it gives the offense the ball back (14-4-3). The offense's foul: from the previous spot when the foul or the basic spot is behind the line; otherwise from the spot of the foul when it is behind the basic spot, and from the basic spot when it is beyond it; a safety, if the defense elects, when the foul is in its own end zone (§4.2), and a safety when the ball is dead there, wherever the foul was; from the goal line when it fouls in the defense's end zone before a touchdown (14-3-6). The offense's foul: from the previous spot, or, behind its own goal line, a safety if the defense elects (14-4-5; §4.2). Before a turnover (an interception, a lost fumble or backward pass, or a failed fourth down, which is a change of possession, 3-35-3), the offense's personal foul leaves the defense the ball, and the yards come off from the dead-ball spot; from the spot of the defense's catch or recovery if it loses the ball again, and it still keeps it (14-4-3, 14-4-5, 14-4-6). On a failed fourth down this applies during a run, the run after a catch included (14-4-3), and to a foul before the throw (14-4-5 Exc. 5); while the pass is in the air, whether the offense is "losing possession during a pass play" (14-4-5 Exc. 5) is OPEN in the rulebook: step 0 offers two choices, the previous spot with the offense's ball and the down repeated (14-4-5, 14-1-2 Item 1), or 14-2-4's dead-ball spot with the defense's ball (§17). The last sentences of 14-4-5 and 14-4-6 also reach the offense's personal foul before a pass thrown from behind the line (or during a fumble or backward pass it recovers) on a play on which the offense "fails to make the line to gain": on fourth down that is the turnover just named; on downs one to three, OPEN in the rulebook (4-8-2 lists the same fouls only before a failed fourth down); after an incomplete pass the ball returns to the previous spot (8-1-4), so step 0 marks the foul from there either way, with the down repeated (14-1-2 Item 1); a catch downed short of the line to gain is refused at step 0; step 6 does it (§17). After the play, or at the instant the ball becomes dead: the succeeding spot, and the down counts (14-4-9); dead-ball fouls by both teams offset there (14-4-9 Item 2). If the team fouled against scores on the play, the yards go to the try, or after a safety to the free kick (14-2-3): refused at step 0; steps 6 and 8 do it (§17). The down: the defense is the team without the ball when it fouls; the offense's foul during the play keeps the down number unless the yards leave the ball beyond the line to gain (then first-and-10); after the play the down counts, with first-and-10 from the new spot if the play made the line to gain (14-1-2 Items 1 and 4, 14-4-9 Item 1); after a turnover, first-and-10 for the team with the ball (14-1-2 Item 6). Tripping is a personal foul (12-2-14), marked the same way; step 0 takes a trip only during the play (§17).

### 4.5 The clock

- **The 10-second runoff** (4-7-1, 4-7-2). After the two-minute warning of either half, a team may not save time by: a foul by either team that stops the clock while the ball is dead (a false start, for instance); intentional grounding; an illegal forward pass; a backward pass thrown out of bounds; spiking or throwing the ball in the field of play after a down (not after a touchdown); or batting or kicking the ball out of bounds illegally (4-7-1). The penalty is 5 yards unless the foul's own is larger (4-7-1 Penalty). When the offense does it while time is in, the officials run 10 seconds off the game clock, set the play clock to 30, and start the game clock on the ready signal; the offense may use a timeout instead, and the clock then starts on the snap. The defense may decline the runoff and keep the yards; declining the yards declines the runoff too, and then the play clock is set to 25 and the clock starts on the snap (4-7-1 Item 1). An offensive substitution foul while the ball is dead and time is in, after the warning, also brings the runoff (4-7-2); repeating it to save time is unsportsmanlike conduct, 15 yards (12-3-1 (m)). After the warning, with the clock running, an offense not set together for a full second commits a false start (7-4-2 Item 6), so the runoff applies to it. There is no runoff with the clock stopped, none for a live-ball foul not on the list (holding, for instance), and never one against the defense (4-7-1 Items 1 and 2; 4-5-4 Notes). A runoff can end the half: the clock runs to zero when 10 seconds or less are left (4-5-4 Notes and 15-5-2 say so for the other runoffs). The defense's time-saving foul after the warning: the play clock is set to 40 and the game clock starts on the ready signal, unless the offense picks the snap; the defense may use a timeout instead (4-7-1 Item 2). In the last 40 seconds of a half, such a foul by the defense while time is in ends the half, unless the defense has a timeout left, or the offense chooses the clock on the snap or to play on after it starts on the ready signal (4-7-3); after time ran out during the down there is no time in (3-36-3), and 4-7-3 does not apply. With time in and the defense out of timeouts, refused at step 0; the step that brings timeouts into the engine does it (§17). ## 5. The officials

### 5.1 One roll per foul, and one per near-foul

When a player commits a foul, the officials roll **once**: flagged or not (`official_sees`, new `CheckKind`, §10). When a player **nearly** commits one (his own foul roll only just made its target, §5.3 and §16 A2) (amended 2026-11-01, step 1), they roll once for a wrong flag (`phantom_flag`). A snap with no foul and no near-foul rolls nothing for officials, which is ADR-005's die only when it matters. Both rolls go through the seeded PRNG (Iron Rule 4).

*Step 1's reading: §16 A9 (added 2026-11-01, step 1).*

### 5.3 Wrong flags come from close plays

A wrong flag in a real game is almost never on nothing at all: it is on a block that looked like a hold, a pass break-up that looked early, a lineman whose hand twitched. So the foul roll of each moment (§8) reports its margin: a foul is a foul roll that misses (margin below 0), as in ADR-091 §5.6's "the first miss is defensive offside", and a **near-foul** is one that only just makes its target, by **10 points or less** (margin 0 to 10, 11 faces; §16 A2 and A9) (amended 2026-11-01, step 1). The officials then roll `phantom_flag` against a chance set so that, league-wide, wrong flags are the owner's share of all flags in that family (**Q2, ruled by the owner 2026-10-31 (§15): 1 in 20**; the choices were 1 in 50, 1 in 20 and 1 in 10). Plain fouls take a tenth of that share (a lineman who did not move is rarely flagged for a false start).

**What the real numbers say (added 2026-10-31; sources P2, P4, P5, P9, Table 9).** No NFL source counts wrong flags across all fouls, but four numbers bound the share. The league's grading found **fewer than one wrong call a game in 2014** (P2), under 7% of that season's flags. The league judged **2 of 14 roughing-the-passer flags wrong** on Week 1's Sunday in 2018, a weekend with twice the usual roughing calls, and its Competition Committee found **3 of more than 80** roughing calls from 2022 "questionable" (P5). **Replay took away 3 of 2019's 437 pass interference flags** (0.7%), a floor, since only 27 flags were reviewed and only a clear error could be reversed (Table 9). In the NBA's audited last two minutes, **about 3% of whistled fouls are wrong** (P9). At **1 in 20** judgment flags the game's wrong flags are about 0.4 a game, **about 3% of all flags**, inside every bound; 1 in 50 gives about 1.2%, and 1 in 10 about 6% (Table 10), close to the league's ceiling and twice what the audits find. **The recommendation stayed 1 in 20, now on these numbers, and the owner ruled it.** Every source also finds missed fouls far outnumbering wrong flags: replay added 7 pass interference flags for each one it took away (Table 9), and the NBA's audit finds 6.7 missed fouls per wrong whistle (P9). With Q1 at 2 in 3 and Q2 at 1 in 20, the design's missed fouls outnumber its wrong flags about 8 to 1 (Table 10).

A wrong flag is a real `PENALTY`, enforced like any other. On the stream it points to the near-foul's roll, and no `FOUL` fact stands behind it, so the film shows what really happened (§7).

### 5.4 Missed and wrong flags sum to the real called rate

The seen chance and the wrong share decide the true rate (§5.2), never the called rate: **the called rate is the target, under any answer to Q1 and Q2.** Each family's foul moment (§8) is tuned so that its called rate lands on Table 1. So Q1 and Q2 change how often fouls happen on the field and how many go unflagged, not how many flags a game has.

### 7.1 Challenges and the booth: the NFL's rules (checked against the 2026 rulebook with §4)

These four bullets were REPORTED on 2026-10-31 (P10) and checked against the 2026 rulebook on 2026-11-14 with §4 (§4.1): two were incomplete and two differed, and all four are corrected here. The rulebook holds only the 2026 rules, so the history in them stays REPORTED (P10). Nothing for challenges or the booth is built at step 0 (Q8's design, §12).

- A team has **two challenges** a game and gets a **third if at least one of its first two wins**, but never a fourth (15-1-1; the rulebook's sentence breaks off after "at least one of its", so "first two" is this ADR's reading). Each challenge needs a timeout left, and one that fails **costs a timeout**, or two if the team had just taken a timeout before it (15-1-1, 15-6-1). A challenge wins when any reviewable part of the ruling is changed, except that a challenge of the line to gain wins only when the first-down ruling changes (15-6-1, 15-3-7). The head coach, or the coach's pregame challenge designee, throws the red flag before the next legal snap or kick (15-1-1). A team whose own foul prevents the next snap can no longer challenge the play before it; the other team still can, and both benefit (15-1-1). A challenge the team may not make (none left, or a play only the booth may review) is charged as a team timeout; a team out of timeouts that throws the flag is penalized 15 yards as a foul between downs, enforced on top of any other foul (15-1-1, 14-1-2 Item 4 Exc., 14-1-3). The history (a third challenge since 2024, when before both had to win; the designee since 2026) is REPORTED (P10). **CORRECTED:** adds what counts as a win, the second timeout, the team whose foul prevents the snap, and the flag a team may not throw; the flag is thrown before the next legal snap or kick.
- **Only the replay official or the league's officiating office may start a review** (they may; they need not) of: a play that **begins after the two-minute warning** of either half; any play in **overtime**; every play on which **points are scored**; every Try; and every ruling of an **interception**, a fumble or backward pass **recovered by the opponent** or out of bounds through the opponent's end zone, a **failed fourth down**, the kicking team's possession at the end of a kick, or a **disqualification** (15-1-2). They may review such a play even when a foul on it would wipe out the ruling (15-1-2). A coach may not challenge these: a coach who throws the flag on one is charged a team timeout (15 yards as a foul between downs with none left), and the flag brings no review of its own (15-1-1, 15-1-2). The booth may start a review only until the next legal snap or kick (15-1-2). A timeout taken after the two-minute warning is given back if a review changes the ruling (15-6-2). The warning comes at the end of the last down snapped before 2:00 (3-41), so a play snapped before it stays the coach's to challenge, even if it ends inside two minutes (15-1-2). **CORRECTED:** "inside the last two minutes" is a play that begins after the warning; the booth may review scores and turnovers, and need not; the list adds every Try, the failed fourth down and disqualifications; and a coach's flag on such a play costs a timeout.
- A ruling is changed only when the league's officiating office finds **clear and obvious visual evidence** (15-2-1). After the two-minute warning of either half, a review that reverses the ruling, or a foul called while the clock was running and later picked up, brings the **10-second runoff** when the correct ruling would not have stopped the clock, or would have restarted it before the next snap (4-7-4): the clock is reset to when the play should have ended, 10 seconds run off, the play clock is set to 30, and the clock starts on the ready signal; with fewer than 10 seconds left, the half (or the game) is over (15-5-2, 4-7-4). **Neither team may decline this runoff**, unlike §4.5's, but either may spend a timeout to avoid it, and the clock then starts on the snap (4-7-4, 15-5-2 Note). 15-5-2 gives this runoff for plays that begin after the two-minute warning and 4-7-4 for a review after it, so for a play snapped before the warning and reviewed after it the rulebook does not say whether 10 seconds run off (OPEN in the rulebook). A reversal, or a live-ball flag picked up, that brings no runoff and no timeout resets the play clock to 40 (4-6-3). When a ruling is changed, the clock goes back to when the ball should have been dead and restarts as Rule 4, Section 3 says; it is not reset if the ruling stands (15-5-1). After a changed ruling, fouls after the ball should have been dead are dropped, except personal and unsportsmanlike conduct fouls; fouls before the snap made before the review are ignored unless they are personal or unsportsmanlike conduct fouls; and the head coach may change the accept-or-decline choice (15-7-3). **CORRECTED:** the evidence is visual, not video; "after the warning", not "inside" it; the picked-up flag; the runoff cannot be declined; and what a changed ruling does to the clock and to the fouls.
- **What can be challenged** (15-3): rulings of fact: a catch or an incompletion; a forward pass or a fumble; a recovery; down by contact, and whether the passer threw before being down; a touch of a pass, a kick or a loose ball; and the goal line (breaking the plane, momentum). The sideline, with limits: a runner ruled inbounds; a runner ruled out of bounds, only for the ball's spot against the line to gain or the goal line and for a fumble or the line reached before two more steps; a receiver; a passer; a loose ball (15-3-5). The dead-ball spot **only against the line to gain** (a challenge wins only if the first-down ruling changes), never just to move the ball closer to or farther from the goal line (15-3-7, 15-3-4); but the spot of a forward fumble out of bounds, or of a fumble on fourth down, after the two-minute warning or on a Try, is reviewable regardless (15-3-2). Also reviewable: whether a pass was illegally thrown (from beyond the line, after the ball crossed it and came back, a second forward pass, a pass after a change of possession), whether a pass was forward or backward, and an illegal forward handoff (15-3-11); the number of players on the field at the snap (15-3-8); penalty administration (the down, the yards, the fouling player's number, the spot of a foul; 15-7-1); and the game clock, only to put time back (15-3-9). **Most fouls cannot be reviewed** (15-4): grounding, an ineligible man downfield, illegal contact, an illegal block, an illegal bat or kick, and anything not listed. But a changed ruling removes a foul built on it (a pass touched noticeably before the interference removes pass interference; a kick touched before the contact removes running into or roughing the kicker), and can create one the reviewable fact makes (an illegal forward pass from beyond the line), or grounding or roughing the kicker after the Referee's pre-review announcement (15-7-2, 15-3-2, 15-3-3). Among the matters 15-9 lists ("including, but not limited to"), the booth's **replay assist** may help pick up a called flag for: roughing the passer called only for a hit to the head or neck when there was no such contact; intentional grounding when the passer was out of the pocket; unnecessary roughness called only for a hit on a runner out of bounds who was not, or for a hit to a defenseless player's head or neck when there was none; a face mask with no hand contact; a horse collar with no collar grab; tripping with no leg contact; and roughing or running into the kicker when the defender touched the kick. Each needs clear and obvious video evidence (15-9). Whether replay assist can add a flag the rulebook does not say, since 15-9 also lets it help on objective facts such as the timing or spot of a foul and where the ball or a player was against a line or the pocket (OPEN in the rulebook; §15's Q8 ruling has the game's replay assist only pick flags up). Separately, the league office may have a player ejected for a flagrant act not called on the field, and that brings a 15-yard penalty (19-2). The history (pass interference challengeable only in 2019; replay assist's fouls since 2024 and 2025) is REPORTED (P10); under the 2026 rule pass interference cannot be reviewed, except when a touch removes it (15-4, 15-3-3). **CORRECTED:** the lists; the limits on the sideline and the spot; the fouls a changed ruling removes or creates; replay assist's list and its conditions; and "never adds one" is not in the rulebook.

**The real numbers (COMPUTED, the real-numbers page, Table 11; nflverse 2013-2025, regular season):** coaches challenged **0.48 to 0.56 times a game** in 2021-2025, both teams together (0.62 over 2013-2025), and won **45%** (38% to 52% by season); every lost challenge in the data shows a timeout charged. The booth reviewed **0.54 to 0.71 times a game** in 2021-2025 and changed **66% to 78%** of what it looked at. Coaches challenged catches (pass completion 619 times, incomplete pass 419), the line to gain (first down 291, short of it 164), down by contact (241), the goal line (60), and fouls almost never: pass interference only in 2019 (54 defensive, 27 offensive, 13 changed) and too many players on the field (19, 17 changed).

## 8. Where fouls come from (outline; each family's design comes at its step)

|---|---|---|

### A3. The lean, its weights, the ceiling and the floor

### A6. The weekly assignment

**Owner's word: not needed.** These are the details of Q3's ruled rule.

- **The rule** (CHOSEN, ruled, Q3): "no crew working the same team twice in four weeks". Step 1 reads it this way: a crew that works a game with team t in week o may not work t in weeks o + 1 to o + 3. Calendar weeks count, byes included.
- **The weeks** (CHOSEN): the 18 regular-season weeks, then playoff week w as week 18 + w. So the rule runs from week 18 into the wild-card round.
- **One game a week per crew** (CHOSEN).
- **Each season starts clean** (CHOSEN): the save keeps only this season's assignments (F7).
- **It can always be met** (COMPUTED, by Hall's theorem). Each game has at most 6 barred crews, so at least 11 are free. Each crew is barred from at most 6 games. So any 1 to 11 games have at least 11 crews between them, and any 7 to 16 games have all 17. The same count still proves "twice in five weeks". For "twice in six" it leaves sets of 8 to 10 games uncovered, so a six-week rule is not guaranteed.
- **The draw** (CHOSEN). Each week, the crews with the fewest games so far come first, with ties broken by a seeded key. Then the week's games are matched to the crews in that order, by augmenting paths. A first-come pick in the same order fails in 3.9% of weeks; the matching never fails (COMPUTED, by simulation). Each week draws exactly 17 numbers, whatever the matching does, so the draw never depends on its own outcome (Iron Rule 4).
- **The workload** (COMPUTED). 272 regular-season games make 16 a crew. Every week 1 to 3 crews sit (franchise's schedule has 16 games in weeks 1 to 4 and 15 to 18, 14 in weeks 5 to 10, and 15 in weeks 11 to 14): 34 crew-weeks a season, 2 a crew. The real league's referees work 15.98 games a season (COMPUTED: 815 games ÷ 3 seasons ÷ 17 referees, REAL).
- **When a week is drawn** (CHOSEN): on its first game day, before its games. It is also drawn when a run stops inside the week, so a save made in game week holds that week's crews (F7: the coaches see their crew in game week). So on days 1 to 6 a straight run has not drawn the week yet, while a run stopped there has. Nothing reads a crew on those days at step 1. ### A9. The wrong-flag chance

**Owner's word: not needed now. Step 2 brings the plain wrong-flag miss below with its numbers.**

### B2. On the line to gain

**Owner's word: shown, not asked; it stands unless the owner says otherwise.**

When the enforcement of the offense's foul leaves the ball exactly on the line to gain, step 0 gives first-and-10. 14-1-2 speaks only of a ball behind the line to gain (Item 1: the down stays) and in advance of it (Item 4: first-and-10). Step 0 reads "on" as a first down, as the rulebook has it wherever it says: a series is won by a ball dead "at or beyond the line to gain", or by the defense's foul that places it there (7-3-1), and Item 5's exceptions give a first down when the yards place the ball "on or in advance of" it. The build plan lists this among the rules; the check found the words open, so it is a reading here. Alternative: the down repeated, as for a ball short of the line.

### B3. The safety election

**Owner's word: shown, not asked; it stands unless the owner says otherwise.**

The offense's live-ball foul behind its own goal line during the play is "a safety if the defense elects to enforce the penalty at that spot" (14-2-2, 14-3-6, 14-4-5, 3-28). Step 0 offers the defense three choices: the safety, the general rule's spot (the previous spot), or decline. "Elects" implies a choice, but the rulebook names no other spot. Alternative: the safety or decline only. It is not offered for intentional grounding thrown from the end zone, whose penalty is the safety itself (8-2-1: accept or decline); nor for a foul at the snap (the previous spot, 14-4-1); nor for a dead-ball foul (the succeeding spot, 14-4-9). A run whose dead-ball spot is in A's end zone is a safety whatever the foul (14-3-6), so accepting equals declining, and step 0 offers the decline (B18). When B ends the down with the ball, A's personal foul goes by B8: the safety stands beside B8's accepts on a failed fourth-down run and while the pass is in the air on one, but before an interception, and before the throw on a failed fourth down, the build offers only B's ball from the dead-ball spot, with no safety (the build plan's one option there).

### B4. Grounding's 10 yards

**Owner's word: shown, not asked; it stands unless the owner says otherwise.**

When the spot of the pass is neither more than 10 yards behind the previous spot nor more than half the distance to the goal line, and 10 yards would be more than half the distance, step 0 cuts the 10 to half the distance (14-2-1). 14-2-1 names grounding as an exception and points to 8-2-1, which does not say. The uncut 10 can carry a ball thrown in the field of play into A's end zone, while 8-2-1 keeps the end zone for a pass thrown from there (the safety). So the ball goes to the spot of the pass, or 10 yards back cut to half the distance, whichever is worse for the offense: snapped at the offense's own 8 and thrown from its 5, the ball goes to its 4, loss of down. A pass thrown exactly half the distance back (there, from its own 4) is not "more than half the distance" (8-2-1), so it is this case too: the same yard line, and the record says the yards were halved (fixed by the review). Alternative: the full 10 yards.

### B5. 15 against 5

**Owner's word: not needed; a reading of the rulebook's text.**

14-5-1's first exception marks the 15 from the previous spot (its words, live or dead-ball) when the other team's foul is "a five-yard penalty without an automatic first down, a loss of down, or a 10-second runoff (15 yards versus five yards), or that is not a spot foul". Step 0 reads "or that is not a spot foul" as one more condition on the five-yard foul: it must also not be a spot foul. Alternative (the either-or reading): any five-yard foul that is not a spot foul is minor, even with an automatic first down, a loss of down or a runoff, which would make the defense's holding minor against a 15-yard foul. The minor foul is SUPERSEDED (the rulebook's "disregarded"), and the 15 is ENFORCED with no coach's decision (B21).

### B6. The offense's personal foul before a pass that falls short

**Owner's word: not needed; the open case is refused until step 6.**

14-4-5's fifth exception ends: "This also applies to a personal foul or unsportsmanlike conduct foul by the offense prior to a forward pass thrown from behind the line when the offense fails to make the line to gain" (14-4-6 says the same of fumbles). On fourth down that is a turnover, and step 0 marks the foul from the dead-ball spot, B keeping the ball (B8). On downs one to three the words would also mark it from the dead-ball spot, while 4-8-2 names the same fouls only before "the offensive team failing to reach the line to gain on fourth down". Step 0 builds what both readings agree on: after an incomplete pass the ball returns to the previous spot (8-1-4), so both readings give the same yard line, and it marks the foul from there, the down repeated (14-1-2 Item 1). A completed pass downed short of the line to gain on downs one to three is refused (B19). Alternatives: the dead-ball spot on every down (the words), or the sentence read as fourth down only (4-8-2).

### B7. A dead-ball foul by the team that ends the down with the ball

**Owner's word: shown, not asked; it stands unless the owner says otherwise.**

After an interception, a touchback on one, or a failed fourth down, a dead-ball foul by either team is marked from the succeeding spot, the down counts, and the team with the ball has first-and-10 after the yards (14-1-2 Item 6; after a failed fourth down, 14-4-9 and 7-3-2, since Item 6 covers only a change of possession during a down). For B's dead-ball foul after a failed fourth down the rulebook is split: 14-4-9 ("the down counts") and 7-3-2 give B the ball, while 14-1-2 Item 5 and 7-3-1 give A a first down after the defense's foul. Step 0 reads it as the down counting: B's ball, the yards marked against B, first-and-10 for B. B's taunt during the play before B intercepts is the same case, since a taunt is a dead-ball foul whenever it happens (14-4-9): the interception stands. Alternative: Item 5's first-and-10 for A.

### B8. A failed fourth down is a change of possession

**Owner's word: not needed; the rulebook's definition.**

3-35-3 lists "When the offensive team fails to reach the line to gain on fourth down" among the changes of possession. Step 0 applies it for 14-4-3 (A's foul during a run followed by a change of possession), for 14-2-4 (the dead-ball spot when the offended team has the ball at the end of the down), for 14-5-2 (a double foul with a change of possession), and for the clock (it starts on the snap after a change of possession, 4-3-2, 4-4). Alternative: only changes made by a catch or a recovery. So, as ruled after the join (2026-11-14; ruling 9, B23) and built, A's personal foul on a failed fourth down goes:

- **During a run, or the run after a catch:** one accept: B keeps the ball, first-and-10, with the yards from the dead-ball spot (14-4-3 bullet 2: "the defense retains possession, and enforcement is from the dead ball spot"; for the run after a catch, 14-4-5 Exc. 5 too). 14-2-4 adds nothing, its spot being the same, and no option gives A the ball back; the safety stands beside it when the foul was behind A's own goal line (B3). A's other fouls on such a run keep the three-and-one with A's ball, and B keeps the ball only by declining (14-4-3).
- **Before the throw:** one accept, the same: B's ball, first-and-10, the yards from the dead-ball spot (14-4-5 Exc. 5, its first and last sentences); after an incomplete pass the ball returns to the previous spot (8-1-4), so the yards come off from there.
- **While the pass is in the air:** two accepts: the previous spot, A's ball and the down repeated (14-4-5's general rule, since Exc. 5's last sentence reaches only a foul "prior to a forward pass"; 14-1-2 Item 1); or 14-2-4's dead-ball spot, B's ball, first-and-10. Whether Exc. 5's first sentence ("prior to the offense losing possession during a pass play") reaches a failed fourth down, which would leave one accept with B's ball, the rulebook does not say: step 0 reads it as not reaching it. Alternative: one accept, B's ball from the dead-ball spot.

§4.3 note 11 says the same.

### B9. The equitable spot, and the end of the run

**Owner's word: not needed.**

- **The equitable spot:** 12-3-1's penalty for acts (a) to (h) is 15 yards "from the succeeding spot or whatever spot the Referee, after consulting with the crew, deems equitable". Step 0 does not model the Referee's judgment and always uses the succeeding spot. Alternative: a spot the caller names.
- **The end of the run:** when the defense's pass interference is also a personal foul and the interference is declined, 8-5's penalty marks the personal foul "from the end of the run" (8-5 Penalty), which is not one of the rulebook's spots (§4.2, §4.3 note 7). Step 0 reads it as 14-4-5's spot for the defense's personal foul before a completion: the previous spot or the dead-ball spot, whichever is better for the offense (14-4-5 Exc. 4, 14-1-3; confirmed after the join, and listed among the build plan's readings). Alternative: the dead-ball spot alone.

### B10. A touchdown with no spot for the defense's foul

**Owner's word: shown, not asked; it stands unless the owner says otherwise.**

When A scores a touchdown on a run (or the run after a catch) and B committed a live-ball foul that is not a personal foul, with the basic spot in B's end zone, step 0 offers the touchdown alone. 14-2-3 gives the offended team the penalty instead of the points "in all scoring situations", but the three-and-one marks B's foul from the basic spot, behind B's goal line, and 14-2-2 names a spot only for the defense's foul behind the offense's goal line and the offense's foul behind the defense's. Alternative: the penalty from the goal line, by likeness to 14-2-2's third bullet. B's personal foul on the scoring down goes to the try, and is refused (B19).

### B11. Clean hands: the word for the other team's flags

**Owner's word: not needed.**

When the team that last gained the ball has clean hands (14-5-2), it "will keep the ball after enforcement for its foul". When A's foul before the change is a personal foul (14-5-2's third exception), or A's foul is a dead-ball foul after it (14-5-3), the fouls offset and B keeps the ball where its own foul would be marked from, with no yards: built. Of A's other live-ball flags the rulebook says nothing more. Step 0 records them as SUPERSEDED, the contract's word for a flag set aside because the other team's flag was enforced instead, and B's flag as ENFORCED with no coach's decision (B21). Alternative: OFFSET. 14-5-2's second exception ("If a safety results from the enforcement of a foul by Team B, the down is replayed at the previous spot") cannot happen at step 0 (half the distance never reaches the goal line, and live-ball flags after the change are refused); it is kept, untested, for the returns step.

### B12. An interception downed in the end zone

**Owner's word: not needed.**

An interception caught in B's own end zone and downed there is a touchback (3-37: the ball dead on or behind the goal line a team defends, the impetus from an opponent): B's ball, first-and-10 at its 20. A catch in the field of play carried back into the end zone (a safety unless momentum, 11-5-1) is the returns step's, and a caller never passes it. B's dead-ball foul after the touchback is marked from the succeeding spot, B's 20 (14-4-9): step 0 reads 14-4-4, which speaks of "a foul by either team after a change of possession" with the dead-ball spot in B's end zone, as being about live-ball fouls during a return. Alternative for that foul: 14-4-4's spots (in the end zone, the 20; in the field of play, the spot of the foul or the 20, whichever is worse for B). A's personal foul before such an interception is marked from B's 20, not the goal line (14-2-2): B's ball, first-and-10 at its 35.

### B13. The last two minutes in overtime

**Owner's word: not needed.**

7-4-4's third bullet (defensive offside with the offense set is a neutral zone infraction "during the last two minutes of a half") and 7-4-2 Item 6 (an offense not set for a full second after the two-minute warning, with the clock running, commits a false start) name the half and its warning. Step 0 applies both in regular-season overtime too, where "the general provisions for the fourth quarter of a game, including timing, shall apply" (16-1-3). Alternative: not in overtime.

### B14. Three clock readings

**Owner's word: not needed.**

- **The last five minutes:** 4-3-2 starts the clock on the snap after a foul "inside the last five minutes of the second half". Step 0 reads that to include regular-season overtime, where "the general provisions for the fourth quarter of a game, including timing, shall apply" (16-1-3): the overtime period's own last five minutes (300 seconds or less left in it), as in the fourth period. Alternatives: the fourth period only; or, in overtime, the whole period.
- **After a penalty that changes possession** (a loss of down on fourth down, for one): the clock starts on the snap, as after any change of possession (4-4, 4-3-2). Alternative: as if the foul had not occurred.
- **The play clock after a declined or offset flag:** 25, as one of 4-6-2's "administrative stoppages", a list "not limited to" the penalty enforcements it names. Alternative: the 40 of a down with no foul (4-6-1).

Not a reading but a rule, ruled after the join (2026-11-14; ruling 10, B23): after a flagged down in which time in the period ran out, the clock starts on the snap, in every period. Without the flag the period would end, and the next one follows an intermission, which is a timeout (3-36-1: a timeout "includes the intermission (4-1-2 and 4-1-3)"); 4-3-2: "Following any timeout (3-36-1), the game clock shall be started on a scrimmage down when the ball is next snapped". An untimed down runs no clock, and is recorded as starting on the snap. A runoff that ends the period keeps the ready signal and play clock 30 (4-7-1), for the record only.

### B15. The offense's dead-ball foul after the play

**Owner's word: not needed.**

14-1-2 Item 4's second sentence: "It is also first-and-10 after enforcement for a Dead Ball Foul (Section 4, Article 9) by Team A at the end of a play from scrimmage when there has not been a change of possession." Read alone, it gives first-and-10 after every such foul. Step 0 reads it with its heading ("Ball in Advance of Line to Gain") and with 14-4-9 Item 1 ("after a down in which Team A has made a first down"): first-and-10 only when the play made a first down; otherwise the down counts, so 3rd-and-10, an incomplete pass, then A's taunt is 4th-and-25. Alternative: first-and-10 after every such foul.

### B16. Taunts, fouls between downs, and 15 against 5's spot

**Owner's word: not needed; written as rules, with their citations.**

- **A taunt** is a dead-ball foul whenever it happens (3-13-1: "or a taunting foul that occurs at any time"; 14-4-9), 15 yards from the succeeding spot (12-3-1 (c), and 12-3-1's penalty; B9 on the equitable spot). Step 0 takes a taunt flagged at any of the four moments. At the snap or during the play it is that down's dead-ball foul, marked as after the play, and the play stands; before the snap it is the next down's foul between downs. It never makes a free play, and it is never asked for a phase (one given is ignored); it is never the same act as a pass interference (B20), and it combines with the down's other flags as any dead-ball foul does (14-4-9 Item 3).
- **A foul between downs** is marked on its own from the succeeding spot and never combines with another foul (14-4-9), so step 0 takes it as the next down's foul before the snap.
- **15 against 5:** the 15 is marked from the previous spot (14-5-1; B5).

### B17. The computer's choice

**Owner's word: CHOSEN, ruled in part (§15, 2026-11-14 02:45Z: the fourth-down rung); the rest is §13's confirmation.**

The order of §4.6, rung by rung, from the chooser's side: points; the half's end; the ball; a first down; the down; yards; the clock; ties. Rungs 1, 3, 4 and 6 are §4.6's order of 2026-10-31 (ADR-091's rule, widened). Rung 5 is the owner's ruling for the team without the ball (the other team's offense facing fourth down), and ADR-091's rule kept for the team with the ball (the lower down). The owner's rung decides only between an option that leaves the other team at fourth down and one that does not: "Yards still decide when neither choice brings fourth down" (the card; restated during the build, 2026-11-14, where the code had widened it to any higher down). Rungs 2 and 7 (the half's end and the clock) are CHOSEN, so the computer neither throws away a last play nor gives the other team time it does not need. Rung 2 reads only the second period, the fourth and overtime: in the first and third periods a next snap comes either way, so the lower rungs decide whether to extend one (a reading after the join). Whether the chooser wants another play is judged on the option that keeps the period going, and after an option that scores, the team that will have the ball is the one that receives the next free kick (the scorer after a safety, 11-5-2: "the team scored upon must put the ball in play by a free kick"; the team scored upon after a touchdown, 11-3-4), the lead counting the option's own points, the try not counted (ruled after the join, 2026-11-14). Rung 7 decides between two options of the same kind, two scores included (the review): so in the fourth period a defense one point down that accepts grounding thrown from the offense's end zone (a safety, with or without the runoff) leads by one after it and receives the kick, and takes the runoff. Rung 8's first tie-break is ADR-091's rule ("gained more yards than the penalty would give": equal yards take the penalty). The last tie-break is a fixed order of the options' ids, each id taken with its flags sorted, so that an exact tie does not depend on the order the flags came in (decided during the build, 2026-11-14). The choice throws on an empty list of options, returns the only option when no one chooses, and gives the same pick when the options or the flags are reordered. Tests: ADR-091's case (3rd-and-10, a free play gaining 7: the penalty), the owner's case (3rd-and-10, a hold on an incomplete pass: the defense declines, 4th-and-10), a runoff at 0:07 (the defense takes it), the scoring options of rungs 2 and 7 (a safety from grounding and a touchdown, each with either team ahead), and each rung once.

### B18. The options step 0 leaves out or folds

**Owner's word: CHOSEN, but for the timeout (a gap, B21); shown, not asked; each stands unless the owner says otherwise.**

- **The untimed down is folded into accepting, in every period:** accepting B's live-ball foul on a period's last down also takes the untimed down (4-8-2). At the end of a half, an offense that wants the half to end declines, which leaves the field as accepting without the extension would (the half ends, and the yards do not matter). In the first and third periods, accepting without the extension (the yards enforced before the next period starts, 4-8-2) is not offered; declining ends the period with no yards. B's request after A's personal foul before an interception or a failed fourth down is folded the same way, into the accept that leaves B the ball. The first and third periods were first planned as never extended; the build changed that on 2026-11-14, since 4-8-2 lets the offense extend any period and only a double foul never extends those two.
- **14-2-4's dead-ball spot**, for A's personal foul when B ends the down with the ball, is the rule's own spot before an interception, on a failed fourth-down run and before the throw on one, so it adds no option there; it is an option of its own only while the pass is in the air on a failed fourth down (B8). It is not offered for B's personal fouls: in step 0's plays, marking from a spot behind gives a spot behind, after half the distance too, so it is never better for A than the spots of §4.2.
- **An option that gives the same result as declining is the decline**, its flag DECLINED (a play that is a safety either way, for one): the same score, or the same next snap and end, and the same clock; how much of the play counts is not compared. An accept that takes the runoff stays its own option, since declining the yards declines the runoff (4-7-1 Item 1), while its twin without the runoff, whose clock is the decline's, merges when its result is the decline's too (ruled after the join, 2026-11-14).
- **No timeout is ever spent,** a gap rather than a choice: A's timeout instead of the runoff, and B's instead of the clock starting, are not offered until timeouts come into the engine (4-7-1; B21's timeout gap).
- **The clock picks** of the computer's rung 7 (B17).

### B20. The input errors

**Owner's word: not needed.**

A caller's mistake is thrown as an error whose message begins "@ff/engine: rulebook:", never returned as a refusal. A caller must never pass:

- a foul by a team, or at a moment, that step 0's table of fouls does not allow, though the rulebook may: for one, A's horse-collar tackle, or B's clipping, block in the back or illegal forward pass, which come with returns; or a trip after the play, which waits for the step that first emits one (B22);
- a down killed before the snap with a flag not before the snap, with no flag, or with two flags of one team (the caller enforces those one at a time, one call each); a flag before the snap on a down with a play;
- a pass foul on a play with no forward pass, or roughing the passer or illegal contact on a play where they cannot happen; illegal contact on a pass in any phase but before the throw, since the restriction ends when the passer "throws a forward or backward pass" (8-4-7; ruled after the join, 2026-11-14); a foul during the play of a pass with no phase (before the throw, the ball in the air, after the catch), or a phase on any other flag, a taunt's excepted (it needs none, and one given is ignored, B16); a foul after the catch on an incomplete pass;
- the defense's pass interference when the ball is not in the air; the offense's interference, an ineligible man downfield, an illegal forward pass or intentional grounding after the catch (the last two may be flagged before the throw or with the ball in the air, alike, since the throw is the foul: decided during the build); pass interference at a spot not more than one yard beyond the line (8-5-1);
- intentional grounding on a pass that was not incomplete, or thrown from beyond the line ("The pass does not have to be incomplete", 8-2-1: a grounded pass that is caught or intercepted waits for the step that first emits one, B22); a foul that needs a pass from behind the line on a play with a pass thrown from beyond it; an illegal forward pass released at or beyond the defense's goal line, since the passer had already scored (11-2: the ball "on, above, or behind the plane of the opponents' goal line" in a runner's possession; ruled after the join, 2026-11-14);
- a personal foul named as the same act as a defensive pass interference that is not one of the call's flags; the same-act link on a foul that is not personal, or not the defense's (a personal foul "that is also defensive pass interference" is the defense's, 14-1-3; decided during the build), or not during the play with the ball in the air, the time pass interference can happen (8-5-1: "from the time the ball is thrown until the ball is touched"), or on a taunt, a dead-ball foul whenever it happens (14-4-9; decided after the review); two flags with one reference;
- a snap outside the field of play; a line to gain not ahead of the snap or beyond the goal line; a down other than first to fourth, or a period other than the four and overtime; a foul or a dead-ball spot more than 10 yards behind either goal line, or not a finite number; time running out during a down with no play;
- the clock's own cases: after the two-minute warning with the clock running, an illegal shift at the snap (the officials call the false start, 7-4-2 Item 6); in a half's last two minutes, defensive offside at the snap with the offense legally set (the officials call the neutral zone infraction, 7-4-4); a negative clock or timeout count.
